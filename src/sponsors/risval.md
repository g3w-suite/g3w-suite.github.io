# RISVAL

RISVAL was an Alpine seismic risk and vulnerability project under the Italy–France ALCOTRA 2014–2020 programme.

Running from April 2017 to April 2020, RISVAL brought together public authorities and research institutions from both sides of the Alps. Its aim was to improve knowledge of seismic risk and develop tools and procedures for prevention, emergency planning and territorial management.

The project addressed an Alpine area where earthquakes occur relatively frequently, generally at low or moderate intensity, but where destructive events cannot be ruled out. Its work considered how seismic events could affect built-up areas and how local authorities could prepare for emergencies.

One area of activity was the monitoring and characterisation of seismic activity across the border region. This included studying seismic potential, the properties of the ground and its response to earthquakes. Another focus was the assessment of the vulnerability of buildings, including the use of innovative methods.

RISVAL also examined existing emergency planning tools and worked on procedures for managing the period after an emergency. Alongside technical work, the project promoted a culture of prevention through communication aimed at residents, local administrators, technical professionals, students and teachers.

The Autonomous Region of Valle d'Aosta led the partnership. Participants included Regione Piemonte, ARPA Piemonte and French research and public organisations, among them CNRS, the Institut des Sciences de la Terre, BRGM and CEREMA. The project was part of the INTERREG France–Italy ALCOTRA cooperation programme.

Its total budget was nearly €2 million, including approximately €1.69 million from the European Regional Development Fund.
