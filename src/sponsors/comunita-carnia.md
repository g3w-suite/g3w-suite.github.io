# Comunità di Montagna della Carnia

![Comunità di Montagna della Carnia](/img/site-carnia-mountain-community-logo.avif)

The Mountain Community of Carnia brings together municipalities in the Carnia area of Friuli Venezia Giulia.

Carnia is a mountain area in north-eastern Italy. Its municipalities share a territorial context that extends across several Alpine valleys.

The Mountain Community brings together local municipalities in a part of Friuli Venezia Giulia shaped by mountains and valleys. Its institutional role is connected to the area and to the public services and activities shared by its member municipalities. The community's website is an entry point for information about the organisation and its territory.

The Friuli Venezia Giulia regional landscape plan can be explored online through the WebGIS Carnia service, listed among the examples of G3W-SUITE on this site. The map provides access to geographic layers associated with the regional plan, bringing together territorial information in an interactive cartographic view.

The service presents the landscape plan through a published map rather than a static image. Visitors can explore the map in a browser and consult the information associated with its layers. This gives the regional planning material a geographic context that can be navigated at different locations and scales.

The Mountain Community of Carnia supported G3W-SUITE versions 3.6 and 3.10. Version 3.6 brought compatibility with QGIS 3.28 LTR, multilayer search, spatial bookmarks and controls for layer transparency. It also introduced tools for navigating between previous and next map views.

Version 3.10 introduced compatibility with QGIS 3.40 LTR, map annotations and an Elasticsearch-based search tool, alongside improvements to charts and online editing. The two versions reflect different stages in the platform's development, from organising and exploring maps to working with richer search and editing tools.

The regional landscape plan map and the community's institutional site offer two ways to explore this context: one through geographic information and the other through information about the local organisation and its activities.
