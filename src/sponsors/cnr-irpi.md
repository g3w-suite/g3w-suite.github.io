# CNR IRPI

![CNR IRPI](/img/site-cnr.avif)

The CNR Research Institute for Geo-Hydrological Protection studied natural hazards and the protection of land and water. In 2026 it became part of CNR-GEO.

Its research included the study and monitoring of landslides, a widespread natural hazard.

The institute worked on the processes that affect slopes, rivers and groundwater, combining scientific research with the study of hazards and their effects on the territory. Its website presents research projects, laboratories, instruments and services alongside information intended for citizens, schools, public administrations and businesses.

Landslides are one of the topics addressed in its scientific communication. The institute's publications describe monitoring and modelling, the use of satellite imagery and the influence of a changing climate. Other research topics include interactions between surface water and groundwater in mountain catchments and the characteristics of Italian rock formations.

Observations can be made from different perspectives. The institute has described the use of airborne LiDAR and remotely piloted aircraft in earth-science research, as well as satellite observations. Its website also provides access to cartographic resources and citizen-science initiatives, connecting research with information for a wider public.

In 2026, IRPI joined the former CNR institutes IGAG and IGG in CNR-GEO. While the new institute's website was being prepared, the former institutes' websites remained available. The IRPI site offers an entry point to its research, projects, laboratories and scientific resources.

IRPI supported versions 3.4 and 3.9 of G3W-SUITE. Version 3.4 introduced tools for displaying raster and vector time series and improvements to online editing forms. Version 3.9 redesigned the map client and added options for managing editing permissions and navigating search results.

The linked changelogs provide more information about each version and its development. The institute's official website offers further details about its scientific work and the transition to CNR-GEO.
