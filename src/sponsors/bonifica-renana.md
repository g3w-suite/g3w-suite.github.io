# Consorzio della Bonifica Renana

![Consorzio della Bonifica Renana](/img/site-bonifica-renana.avif)

The Consorzio della Bonifica Renana manages drainage channels and works to reduce hydrogeological risk in its territory.

Consorzio della Bonifica Renana has supported multiple G3W-SUITE releases, from version 3.0 through later developments.

Version 3.0 introduced QGIS API integration.

The supported releases track the platform's evolution from QGIS API integration in 3.0 through chart and Atlas printing support in 3.1, layer permissions and expression-based editing in 3.3, and conditional forms and time-series visualisation in 3.4.

Later supported versions added multi-layer search and spatial bookmarks in 3.6, map export to SVG and GeoPDF in 3.8, and map annotations, Elasticsearch search and QGIS 3.40 LTR compatibility in 3.10. Version 3.11 continued compatibility updates with QGIS 3.44 LTR and improvements to map interaction, editing and charts.

Its work spans the plains, hills and mountains of its district.

The consortium is a public body whose district follows the Reno river basin. Its responsibilities differ across the landscape: in the plain, it manages an artificial drainage network and distributes surface water for irrigation and productive uses; in the hills and mountains, it supports projects addressing hydrogeological instability.

The drainage system includes approximately 2,000 kilometres of artificial channels, pumping stations and retention basins. It helps carry away rainwater from the plain, while natural rivers and streams in that area are managed by the Emilia-Romagna regional government. This distinction between artificial drainage infrastructure and natural watercourses is important to understanding the consortium's work.

In the uplands, the consortium works with municipalities, municipal unions and the regional government. At their request it can co-finance, design and carry out interventions against hydrogeological instability, including works that support access and the use of the surrounding environment. Natural public watercourses in these areas remain under regional responsibility.

The consortium's activity is governed by regional law. Property owners within its district contribute to the operation and maintenance of the drainage system, with contributions calculated according to a classification plan. Its governance includes representatives elected by contributing property owners and delegates from municipalities.
